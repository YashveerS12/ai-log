In 1992 a neural network trained on the MNIST dataset could outperform a human expert on digit classification, yet it was only a single hidden layer network with 300 units.  
The network was trained with backpropagation and stochastic gradient descent, using just 20 000 examples, and its error rate was about 0.7 %.  
This early success showed that even simple deep learning models could match human-level performance on specific tasks before the deep learning boom.
